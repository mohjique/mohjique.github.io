Matthew Oh

Economics & CS, Texas A&M · GTM/Deployment, dConstruct Robotics

I work outward from a few fixed points: economics as my grounding in how people, markets, and organizations actually behave, and computer science as the newest lens I'm building to look at the same questions. In practice, that means I gravitate toward roles that sit between the technical and the commercial — reading the numbers, but also getting my hands on the hardware, in the field, with the people who have to make a product actually work.

Most recently that meant a summer as an Operations & GTM Intern at dConstruct Robotics, a Series A robotics startup bringing autonomous reality-capture robots to the construction industry. I split my time between market and competitive analysis for the US launch, and getting hands-on with the handheld scanner and the dASH-er robot itself — running obstacle-avoidance and navigation tests, prepping customer pilots, and troubleshooting hardware issues directly with the engineering team in Singapore. It's the kind of work where you're expected to understand both the deal and the deployment, and I liked living in that overlap.

Before tech pulled me in, I spent several years leading MUN Impact, a UN-affiliated youth organization spanning 171 countries — eventually as Global Co-Secretary-General, running a large, geographically distributed team and representing the org at UN forums including ECOSOC and the UN Water stakeholder consultations. I also served two years in the Singapore Civil Defence Force as a firefighter, and I currently consult pro bono with 180 Degrees Consulting at Texas A&M. What ties it together is a comfort with ambiguity: managing people and stakeholders across time zones, then shipping something concrete anyway.
